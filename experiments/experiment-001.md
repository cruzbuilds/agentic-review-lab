# Experiment 001: Five Critics or One Good Prompt

Full record: [Five-Critics-or-One-Good-Prompt](https://github.com/cruzbuilds/Five-Critics-or-One-Good-Prompt).
This page is a map to it, not a copy of it. Closed 2026-09-19.

## Research question

On the same code, the same model, the same week: does a swarm of narrow specialist reviewers find
materially more real problems than one general reviewer, and what does each one miss? Secondary,
registered before results: does the swarm produce an audit trail a general review does not?

## The subject

A three-tier TypeScript application (Next.js, Prisma, Postgres, hand-rolled JWT auth) generated by a
coding agent from one paragraph in twelve and a half minutes, committed exactly as produced, and
frozen at one commit. Public at [idea-log](https://github.com/cruzbuilds/idea-log), `b38c5b0`.
Nobody reviewed it before the study. No defects were seeded. A specification was written before
generation and withheld from the builder.

## The arms

| Arm | What | Registered | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | One general reviewer, one strong 170-word prompt naming every domain | Yes | 3 reading-only, 3 with tools |
| B | The V1 specialist swarm, five charters, merged verdict | Yes | 3 reading-only, 3 with tools |
| C | One general reviewer, twenty-four words, no domain map | No. Added after A and B were analysed. Labeled as such everywhere. | 3 with tools |

The reading-only condition exists because the author's harness silently denied every tool in the
first six runs. It was kept as a labeled condition rather than discarded ([DEVIATIONS.md](https://github.com/cruzbuilds/Five-Critics-or-One-Good-Prompt/blob/main/DEVIATIONS.md), D-003, D-004).

## Major result

The strong generalist and the swarm tied at 38 confirmed defects. The naive generalist found 30, and
found every merge-blocking defect in every run. Precision was about 80% for all three. The swarm's
advantage was decomposition inside testing and a per-agent inspection record. Its cost was a
structural blind spot: the most consequential defect in the subject was noticed by a swarm agent and
filed as owned by nobody. Four of five sealed predictions failed. The original hypothesis was not
supported, and the architecture changed as a result.

[Findings summary](../findings/experiment-001-summary.md) has the numbers.

## Limitations

One repository, one model family, one week, one author who built one of the arms and judged all
three with open, versioned verdicts and no second judge yet. The subject had no meaningful tests, no
CI and almost no infrastructure, so the test and infrastructure specialists reviewed absence and
their tools had nothing to run. The strong generalist's prompt was written by the swarm's author.
Arm C is one naive prompt, written by someone who had seen the results. No statistics; three runs
describe stability, not significance. Nothing generalizes to multi-agent systems as a class.
